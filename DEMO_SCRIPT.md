## The 2-Minute Demo Script

### Timing Breakdown
- **0:00-0:20** - Hook & Problem (20 seconds)
- **0:20-0:40** - Solution & Product Intro (20 seconds)
- **0:40-1:30** - Live Demo (50 seconds)
- **1:30-2:00** - Impact & Closing (30 seconds)

---

## Full Script (Memorize This)

### [0:00-0:20] The Hook - Pain Point

> **"Did you know that over 60% of African students struggle with traditional textbook learning?"**
> 
> **"Many can't afford books, internet is too slow for videos, and millions have low literacy rates. But here's the thing - everyone can talk and listen. That's where AfriVoice comes in."**

**Body Language:** Make eye contact with judges, use hand gestures to emphasize numbers

**Why This Works:** Starts with a surprising statistic, identifies a real problem, sets up the solution

---

### [0:20-0:40] The Solution

> **"AfriVoice is a voice-first AI tutor built specifically for African students."**
> 
> **"No typing, no reading, no complexity. You just talk to it like you're chatting with a teacher. And it talks back. That's it. Learning through conversation."**

**Body Language:** Smile, show enthusiasm, gesture toward screen

**Why This Works:** Clear value proposition in simple language

---

### [0:40-1:30] Live Demo (The Star of the Show)

**Step 1: Show Dashboard (5 seconds)**
> *[Navigate to dashboard]*
> 
> **"This is my dashboard. I can see my past learning sessions right here."**

**Step 2: Start Session (5 seconds)**
> *[Click "Start Voice Tutor" button]*
> 
> **"One click. That's all it takes."**

**Step 3: Ask Question (10 seconds)**
> *[Wait for mic to activate]*
> 
> **[Speak clearly into microphone]: "Explain blockchain like I'm 10 years old"**

**Step 4: Let AI Respond (20 seconds)**
> *[Let AI speak, don't interrupt]*
> 
> *[Point to screen as transcript appears]*
> 
> **"See how natural that is? It's using African examples - matatus, markets, things students actually know."**

**Step 5: Show Transcript (5 seconds)**
> *[Scroll through transcript]*
> 
> **"Everything is saved automatically. Students can review what they learned."**

**Step 6: End Session (5 seconds)**
> *[Stop session]*
> 
> **"Session saved. It's that simple."**

**Backup Plan:** If audio fails, switch to pre-recorded video: 
> **"Let me show you a quick recording of it in action..."**

---

### [1:30-2:00] Impact & Closing

> **"This solves a massive problem. Students with low literacy can learn. Students with slow internet can learn. Students with cheap phones can learn."**
> 
> **"Because voice works everywhere. It's the most natural interface. And it's accessible to everyone."**
> 
> **"We're making education accessible through conversation. That's AfriVoice."**

**Body Language:** Confident, passionate, make final eye contact with each judge

**Call to Action (if allowed):**
> **"We'd love to show you more. Let's chat after?"**

## Handling Judge Questions

### Expected Questions & Answers

**Q: "How is this different from ChatGPT?"**
**A:** "ChatGPT requires typing and reading. 60% of our target users have low literacy. Voice is accessible to everyone. Plus, we're optimized for African contexts with relevant examples."

**Q: "What's your business model?"**
**A:** "For the MVP, we're focused on proving the concept. Future revenue could come from school partnerships, government programs, or a freemium model. But first, we need to prove students actually learn better this way."

**Q: "How do you measure learning outcomes?"**
**A:** "Great question. Right now we're tracking engagement - session length, questions asked, return rate. Next step is adding quizzes and knowledge checks to measure actual learning."

**Q: "What about internet connectivity?"**
**A:** "Voice uses way less bandwidth than video. A 5-minute session is under 1MB. But you're right - our roadmap includes offline mode where students download lessons over WiFi, then learn offline."

**Q: "How scalable is this?"**
**A:** "Very. Vapi's infrastructure handles millions of calls. Supabase scales automatically. Our biggest cost is AI API calls, but as we grow, we can negotiate better rates or fine-tune our own models."

**Q: "Why voice instead of chat?"**
**A:** "Accessibility. Many African students can't read well or type fast. But everyone can talk. Voice removes the literacy barrier and makes learning feel more natural, like talking to a teacher."

**Q: "What's next after the hackathon?"**
**A:** "Pilot with 5 schools in Kenya. Add subject categories. Build teacher dashboards. Then expand to Nigeria, Ghana, and South Africa. Long-term: WhatsApp integration and local language support."

## Body Language & Presentation Tips

### Do's
- ✅ Make eye contact with all judges
- ✅ Smile and show enthusiasm
- ✅ Speak clearly and at moderate pace
- ✅ Use hand gestures to emphasize points
- ✅ Pause after key statements
- ✅ Show genuine passion for the problem
- ✅ Stand confidently, don't fidget

### Don'ts
- ❌ Rush through the demo
- ❌ Read from notes
- ❌ Turn your back to judges
- ❌ Apologize for "rough edges"
- ❌ Get defensive about questions
- ❌ Talk over the AI during demo
- ❌ Go over 2 minutes

---

## Backup Plans for Technical Failures

### If Internet Dies
1. Switch to pre-recorded video immediately
2. Narrate over the video
3. Say: "We pre-recorded this just in case. Let me walk you through..."

### If Audio Doesn't Work
1. Switch to video with captions
2. Say: "Looks like we're having audio issues, but you can see the transcript here..."
3. Continue with visual demo

### If Website is Down
1. Show video immediately
2. Show screenshots on phone
3. Say: "We're experiencing high traffic, let me show you a recording..."

### Nuclear Option
If everything fails:
1. Apologize briefly (5 seconds)
2. Pivot to problem/solution narrative
3. Show GitHub code on laptop
4. Emphasize the idea and impact over the demo
